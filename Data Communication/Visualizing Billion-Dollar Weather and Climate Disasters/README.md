**Course:** INFO 3312/5312 — Data Communication, Spring 2025

## The Dataset

NOAA Billion-Dollar Weather and Climate Disasters (1980–2024) — a comprehensive record of weather events in the US that caused at least $1 billion in damages, including hurricanes, floods, wildfires, tornadoes, droughts, and winter storms.

---

## The Goal

Design a visualization that emphasizes the emotional and human weight of the data — not just counts and costs, but the story of increasing frequency and severity over time.

---

## Design Considerations

This exercise was explicitly about storytelling over technical precision. A few questions that shaped the approach:

- **What's the most important signal?** The trend of increasing events over time, the clustering of disasters in certain years, or the geographic pattern?
- **What emotion should the chart evoke?** Urgency, cumulative weight, or something else?
- **Color and annotation choices**: Red/orange palettes emphasize heat and danger. Direct annotation of notable events (Hurricane Katrina, 2017 hurricane season, California wildfire years) connects abstract numbers to lived memory.

NOAA's own interactive visualizations served as a reference point — the exercise involved studying what they chose to emphasize and making deliberate choices about what to do differently.

---

## Key Finding

The frequency of billion-dollar disaster events has increased substantially since 1980. What used to be unusual years are now typical ones. The data shows this clearly — the challenge is designing a chart that communicates the magnitude of that shift to a general audience without requiring them to already know the statistics.
